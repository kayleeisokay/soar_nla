The chat-template results show a larger benefit from the final-paragraph rewrite than the raw-text results. With the official layer-32 NLA, single-token query-and-answer substitutions reach 0.475 with the template, compared with 0.635 in raw text; adding the rewrite raises these rates to 0.717 and 0.787. For four-token patching, substitutions reach 0.793 with the template and 0.977 in raw text, while combined edits reach 0.923 and 0.977. Final-paragraph rewrites alone have lower average rates than lexical substitutions in both formats. Donor patching is also less effective with the template, reaching 0.087 for one token and 0.835 for four, compared with 0.805 and 0.970 in raw text. The four-token spans differ across formats: raw text includes the final query token and answer prefix, whereas the chat format uses the assistant header.
